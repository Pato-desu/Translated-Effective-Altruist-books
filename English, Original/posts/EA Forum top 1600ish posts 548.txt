By “significant,” Imean significant relative to our expectation over future suffering.For something to constitute an “s-risk” under this definition, thesuffering involved not only has to be astronomical in scope (e.g.,“more suffering than has existed on Earth so far”), but alsosignificant compared to other sources of expected future suffer-ing. This last bit ensures that “s-risks,” assuming sufficienttractability, are always a top priority for suffering-focusedlongtermists. People have two types of motivation. Many posts onsuffering-focused ethics, such as my article on tranquil-ism, focus mostly on “system-1/ model-free/ impulsivemotivation,” not on “system-2/ model-based/ reflectivemotivation.” The latter type of motivation can attach it-self to different life goals.
