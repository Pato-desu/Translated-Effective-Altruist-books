Core claim1. Assuming moral objectivism (or a close approximation), we are probablyunknowingly guilty of serious, large-scale wrong-doing (“ongoing moralcatastrophe”).II. Deﬁnition: What is a moral catastrophe? Three criteria:1. Must be a serious wrong-doing (closer to wrongful death or slavery thanmild insults or inconveniences).2. Must be large-scale (instead of a single wrongful execution, or a single mantortured)3. Broad swathes of society are responsible through action or inaction (can’tbe unilateral unavoidable actions by a single dictator).III. Why we probably have unknown moral catastrophes. a. Assumption: It’s possible to engage in great moral wrongdoings evenwhile acting in accordance to your own morals, and that of yoursociety.i. Basic motivation: an honest, sincere Nazi still seems to be actingwrongly in important ways.ii. It’s not relevant whether this wrongdoing is due to mistakenempirical beliefs (All Jews are part of a major worldwideconspiracy) or wrong values (Jews are subhuman and have nomoral value).b. Given that assumption in mind, pretty much every major society inhistory has acted catastrophically wrongly.i. Consider conquistadores, crusaders, caliphates, Aztecs etc. whoconquered in the name of God(s), who they called good and just.ii. It’s unlikely that all of these people in history only professed sucha belief, and that all of them were liars instead of true believers.iii. Existence proof: People can (and in fact do) do great evil withoutbeing aware of this.c. Us having ongoing moral catastrophes isn’t just a possibility, butprobable.i. We are not that di!erent from past generations: Literallyhundreds of generations have thought that they actually wereright and had ﬁgured out the One True Moralityii. As recent as our parents’ generation, it was a common belief thatsome people have more rights than others because of race,sexuality etc.iii. We live in a time of moral upheaval, where our morality is verydi!erent from our grandparents’.iv. Even if some generation would eventually ﬁgure out All ofMorality, the generation that gets everything right is probably ageneration whose parents gets almost everything right.2. The Disjunctive Argumenta. Activists are not exempt. Even if all your pet causes come to fruition,this doesn’t mean our society is good, because there are still unknownmoral catastrophes.b. There are so many di!erent ways that a society could get things verywrong, that it’s almost impossible to get literally everything right.i. This isn’t just a minor concern, we could be wrong in ways thatare a sizable proportion of how bad the Holocaust is.
c. There are many di!erent kinds of ways that society could be wrong.i. We could be wrong about who has moral standing.(eg. fetuses,animals)ii. We could be empirically wrong about what harms or hurts peoplewho morally matter (eg. religious indoctrination of children)iii. We could be right about some obligations but not others.i. We can act immorally in paying too much attention andusing resources on false moral obligations (a la crusaders)iv. We could be right about what’s wrong and should be ﬁxed, butwrong at how to prioritize di!erent ﬁxes.v. We could be right about what’s wrong, but wrong about what isand is not our responsibility to ﬁx. (eg. poverty, borders)vi. We could be wrong about the far future (natalism, existentialrisk)d. Within each category, there are multiple ways to go wrong.i. Further, some are mutually exclusive. Eg. Pro-lifers could beright and abortion is a great sin, or fetuses don’t matter and it’sgreatly immoral to deprive women of their freedom in eg. thirdtrimester abortions.ii. Unlikely that we’re currently at the golden mean for all of thesetrade-o!s.e. Disjunction comes into play.i. Even if you believe that we’re 95% right at each major issue, andthere are maybe 15 of them, the total probability that we are rightis maybe ~.95^15~=46% (LZ: Assumes independence)ii. In practice, 95% sure we’re right at each major issue seems waytoo conﬁdent, and 15 items too low.IV. What should we do about it? 1. Discarded possibility: hedging. If you’re not sure, play it “safe”, morallyspeaking.2. Eg. even if you think farmed animals probably aren’t sentient, or sentiencedoesn’t morally matter, you can go vegetarian “just in case”3. This does NOT generally work well enough because it’s not robust: as noted,too many things can go wrong, some in contradictory directions.4. Recognition of Wrongdoinga. Actively try to ﬁgure out which catastrophic wrongs we’re committing
i. Research more into practical ﬁelds (eg. animal consciousness)where we can be critically wrongii. Research more into moral philosophyi. Critical: bad to have increased technological knowledge w/oincreased moral wisdomii. imagine Genghis Khan w/nuclear weaponsiii. These ﬁelds must interacti. Not enough for philosophers to say that animals areimportant if they are conscious and for scientists to say thatdolphins are conscious but don’t know if this isimportant...our society must be able to integrate this.b. Need marketplace of ideas where true ideas win outc. Rapid intellectual progress is critical.i. If it’s worth ﬁghting literal wars to defeat the Nazis or endslavery, it’s worth substantial material investment and societalloss to ﬁgure out what we’re currently doing wrong.5. Implementation of improved valuesa. Once we ﬁgure out what great moral wrongs we’ve committed, wewant to be able to make moral reparations for past harms, or at leaststop doing future harms in that direction as quickly as possible.b. To do this, we want to maximize ﬂexibility in material conditionsi. Extremely poor/war-torn societies would be unable to make rapidmoral changes as neededii. LZ example: Complex systems built along speciﬁc designs are lessresilient to shocks, and also harder to change, cf. Antifragile.iii. In the same way we stock up resources for war preparation, wemight want to save up resources for future moral emergencies, sowe can eg. pay reparations, or at least quickly make the relevantchanges.i. LZ: Unsure how this is actually possible in practice. Eg,individuals usually save by investing, and governments saveby buying other government’s debt or by investing in theprivate sector, but it’s unclear how the world “saves” as awhole.c. We want to maximize ﬂexibility in social conditionsi. Even if it’s materially possible to make large changes, societymight make such changes very di"cult, because inertia andconservatism bias.
ii. Constitutional amendments, for example, are suspect.V. Conclusion/Other remarks1. Counterconsideration One: Building a society that can correct moralcatastrophes isn’t the same as actually correcting moral catastrophes.2. Counterconsideration Two : Many of the measures suggested above toprepare for correcting moral catastrophes may themselves be evila. e.g. money spent on moral research could have instead been spent onglobal poverty, building a maximally ﬂexible society might involvedraconian restrictions on current people’s rights3.
